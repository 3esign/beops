# Belgrade sensory, soundscape and ecological observations

Eight additional primary records: seven scholarly works and one documented artistic field practice. Six have verified DOIs; the bird paper and artistic work have other identifiers. Six scholarly full texts were inspected in relevant methods/results passages; one scholarly record was read through indexed primary sections; the artistic record is its creator's statement on the publisher's page. These are historical observations and interpretive practices, not eight installed sensor networks or live feeds.

Deduplication baseline: the existing 93-entry wide bibliography and the 32-work sensing-context supplement, including all four supporting lane reports. The 2019 urban-noise sensor paper, bee genetics, thermal studies and general soundscape-ecology theory were excluded as already covered. Titles and DOIs were compared locally before handoff.

The added value is a wider definition of an observation chain: a repeatedly inspected tree; a timed bird count; a sound-level meter paired with a phone; a listener walking and documenting a place; a structured interview; or an edited composition whose raw field material remains separate. The meaning of a sound cannot be recovered from its decibel value alone. Conversely, an evocative sound portrait does not establish calibrated exposure or representative public opinion.

## 1. Sixteen years of flowering observations

**Ocokoljić, Mirjana; Petrov, Djurdja; Galečić, Nevenka; Skočajić, Dejan; Košanin, Olivera; Simović, Isidora (2023). “Phenological Flowering Patterns of Woody Plants in the Function of Landscape Design: Case Study Belgrade.” Land, 12(3), 706. Published 2023-03-18. DOI: [10.3390/land12030706](https://doi.org/10.3390/land12030706).**

- Read level: indexed primary abstract, methods, supplementary-material listing and data-availability statement from the [publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-445X/12/3/706). Direct navigation returned 429; no full-page retrieval claim.
- Local observation: 13 woody taxa, 90,860 BBCH phenological observations during **2007–2022**, across 12 Belgrade locations, including urban and suburban settings. Direct visual stage observations are combined with Belgrade/Surčin meteorological records; growing-degree days and trends are derived quantities. Exact visit cadence was not established.
- Data route: article lists a [supplement](https://www.mdpi.com/article/10.3390/land12030706/s1) containing site descriptions and statistical/climatic tables; it was not downloaded. This is not a verified public release of all individual observations.
- Rights: article CC BY; its data statement links public climatology products while also stating that RHMZ data cannot be republished. That unresolved source-specific distinction prevents treating every underlying record as openly reusable.

## 2. Oak leaf-fall as repeated human sensing

**Batos, Branislava; Orlović, Saša; Ninić-Todorović, Jelena; Šešlija Jovanović, Darka; Miljković, Danijela (2016). “Phenological pattern of leaf-fall phenophase in Quercus robur L. populations of the Belgrade area.” Bulletin of the Faculty of Forestry / Glasnik Šumarskog fakulteta, 114, 9–28. DOI: [10.2298/GSF1614009B](https://doi.org/10.2298/GSF1614009B).**

- Read level: [publisher full text, Serbian and English](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/img/doi/0353-4537/2016/0353-45371614009B.pdf), methods and results.
- Local observation: **29 trees per population**, Ada Ciganlija forest park and Bojčin forest; **weekly September–December observations in 2004, 2005 and 2006**. Selected trees were healthy mature specimens, not a random census of all urban trees.
- Method: observers register start, duration and completion of leaf fall; temperature/precipitation comparisons use the Belgrade and Surčin stations. Early/average/late groups and statistical differences are analytical classifications, not directly sensed variables.
- Data/access: figures and tables expose historical summaries. No separate dataset DOI, raw observation repository, sensor model or article-specific reuse licence was established. A public PDF is not evidence of a current phenology service. The two-site design supports local contrasts; extrapolation to the entire city would need additional sampling.

## 3. Bird counts and river habitats

**Simić, Dragan V.; Tucakov, Marko (2004 issue label; accepted 2005-05-09). “Numbers and local movements of Pygmy Cormorants Phalacrocorax pygmeus wintering in Belgrade.” Acrocephalus, 25(123), 207–212. DOI: not established. Persistent record: [URN:NBN:SI:doc-Q2KNZI2R](https://www.dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:doc-Q2KNZI2R).**

- Read level: [publisher-hosted issue PDF](https://www.ptice.si/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/3_5_21_ljubljansko_barje_acro_2004.pdf), article pp.207–212; the national-library record verifies issue metadata. Its 2022 repository publication date is not the study date.
- Local observation: Sava–Danube confluence and adjoining Belgrade river reaches; point counts and observations of birds using river habitats in **1996, January 2001, February–April 2003, and March–April 2004**. Repeated observation points and approximately matched seasonal dates enable limited historical comparison.
- Method/limit: human visual counts, not acoustic species classification or individual telemetry. Optical instrument models are unspecified; sampling is episodic rather than continuous. Detection, roost relocation and observer coverage affect estimates.
- Data/access: published counts and figures; no open raw-count deposit or explicit reuse licence established. Fine roost coordinates are not reproduced here. No present population estimate is inferred.

## 4. A measured soundscape pilot in Block 63

**Stefanović, Jovana; Milenković, Vladimir; Marić, Jelena (2025). “Soundscape as an Urban Transformation Tool / Echoes of New Belgrade.” In Keeping Up with Technologies to Shape Joyful Cities, International Academic Conference on Places and Technologies, 10, 263–270. DOI: [10.18485/arh_pt.2025.10.ch30](https://doi.org/10.18485/arh_pt.2025.10.ch30).**

- Read level: [publisher metadata](https://doi.fil.bg.ac.rs/volume.php?i=30&issue=arh_pt-2025-10&pt=eb_ser) and [full text](https://doi.fil.bg.ac.rs/pdf/eb_ser/arh_pt/2025/arh_pt-2025-10-ch30.pdf), methods and tables.
- Local observation: ten points on the perimeter and within **New Belgrade Block 63**. A BENETECH GM1352 meter and Decibel X iOS app operated together, with at least three readings per device in four time-of-day intervals; weekday/weekend tables report extrema. Calendar campaign dates and phone model are not established.
- Interpretation: completed pilot measurements appear despite future-tense abstract wording. Resident-perception surveys and interventions remain future work. Device comparison alone is not traceable acoustic calibration; the published summaries do not establish continuous exposure or a validated city noise map.
- Data/rights: tables are available in the article; no standalone timestamped dataset found. Publisher metadata explicitly identifies **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**. No raw audio or geotagged personal records collected.

## 5. Skadarlija: listening, performance and preference

**Dumnić, Marija (2017). “How music affects soundscape: Musical preferences in Skadarlija.” Muzikologija, 22, 75–88. DOI: [10.2298/MUZ1722075D](https://doi.org/10.2298/MUZ1722075D).**

- Read level: [publisher full text](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/ft.aspx?id=1450-98141722075D), especially pp.78–79 and analysis.
- Local observation: Skadarlija tavern performances, musicians and audiences. Methods combine **soundwalking, participant observation, and in-depth semi-structured interviews**, with audio and occasional video/photo documentation. The paper situates work in City Sonic Ecology and related research; exact field-recording dates and recording hardware were not established. The cited project period is not substituted for campaign dates.
- Contribution: the observed repertoire and interaction between performers and listeners make the soundscape a social process, relevant to how Beops might describe perceived place and meaning. This is interpretive ethnographic evidence, not a representative poll or calibrated environmental-noise measurement.
- Data/rights: published analysis; no public licensed interview corpus or field-recording dataset identified. Article-specific reuse terms were not established from the inspected PDF. No interviews, faces, voices or music recordings were downloaded. This article's participant material should not be treated as freely reusable because the paper is public.

## 6. Festival soundwalks and a perception questionnaire

**Medić, Ivana (2017). “Years of Sound Living: Mikser Festival in Savamala (2012–2016).” Muzikologija, 22, 39–57. DOI: [10.2298/MUZ1722039M](https://doi.org/10.2298/MUZ1722039M).**

- Read level: [publisher full text](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/img/doi/1450-9814/2017/1450-98141722039M.pdf), methodology, soundwalk narrative and questionnaire passages.
- Local observation: Savamala festival sites; recordings, soundwalks and video documentation during the summers of **2014, 2015 and 2016**, within a wider 2012–2016 festival account. The questionnaire was conducted **February–May 2017**, with 68 respondents in each of two groups. These are distinct observational windows.
- Method/limit: mixed ethnography, interviews, recorded impressions and questionnaire comparisons. Microphone model, audio sampling rate and calibrated levels are unspecified. The study's respondent groups are not a representative sample of Belgrade residents; its discussion also anticipates further perception research.
- Data/rights: questionnaire results and narrative observations are published; no separately licensed response/audio dataset identified. Article-specific reuse terms remain unverified. No recording or participant microdata acquired. The paper is evidence of completed local sensory field practice, not the continuing operation of the festival at those sites.

## 7. Silence and sound as observed public practices

**Hofman, Ana; Atanasovski, Srđan (2017). “Sonic memory interventions against politics of urban silencing.” Muzikologija, 22, 89–101. DOI: [10.2298/MUZ1722089H](https://doi.org/10.2298/MUZ1722089H).**

- Read level: [publisher full text](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/ft.aspx?id=1450-98141722089H), local case discussions and sources.
- Local observation/context: public sonic interventions in **Belgrade and Ljubljana**. The Belgrade account discusses three memorial actions between **July 2015 and February 2017**, including an event on 2015-07-10, and the changing public-space context around Slavija.
- Method/limit: comparative, interpretive analysis of documented public actions, sound/silence, memory and urban politics; no fixed sensor, acoustic calibration, continuous sampling or citywide survey. Ljubljana evidence must not be counted as a Belgrade deployment. Claims about power and commodification are the authors' interpretations. Discussion of the then-proposed musical fountain is not commissioning evidence.
- Data/rights: article and referenced public documentation, not a released sensor dataset. No standalone data licence or article-specific reuse grant established in the inspected text. No public-action participant identities or media files collected. Useful as a question about whose listening and whose silence a sensing system represents.

## 8. Documented artistic field practice: peripheries

**Klement, Katharina (2017). peripheries: Sound portrait Belgrade 2014–2016. Gruenrekorder, Gruen 173; EAN 4050486988459. DOI: not established.**

- Read level: [creator's account and release metadata on the original publisher page](https://www.gruenrekorder.de/?page_id=16044). This is an artistic research/field-practice record, **not a peer-reviewed paper**; reviews on the same page were not used as evidence for methods.
- Local observation: Klement describes **nine weeks in Belgrade in 2014**, excursions collecting environmental sounds, and interviews with people about the city's soundscape. Those materials became an acoustic archive and then a composed sound portrait. The work's 2014–2016 span and 2017 release are separate from the explicitly dated field stay.
- Method/limit: exploratory phonography and interviews followed by compositional selection. Exact recording hardware, sampling rate, interview sample and complete date/location log are not established. An edited composition cannot be interpreted as an unaltered time series or representative city sample.
- Access/rights: commercial release and public descriptive metadata; no open raw archive or explicit reusable audio licence found. No audio playback/download, purchase, interviews or contact undertaken.
