# Sensing beyond dedicated sensors: eight verified foundations

Additive wide-bibliography supplement. These eight records were checked against the existing93-entry bibliography by title/author/topic; they add specific works rather than replace the foundational ontology, Kitchin, Mattern or citizen-sensing entries. They are international methods and conceptual context, **not evidence of deployment in Belgrade**. All web checks below were performed2026-09-06. Metadata/abstract review is explicitly distinguished from full-text reading. Citations and short original annotations are provided; books and articles are not republished.

## T01 — Environmental relations produced through sensing

Gabrys, J. (2016). *Program Earth: Environmental Sensing Technology and the Making of a Computational Planet*. University of Minnesota Press. ISBN 9780816693146. [Primary publisher](https://www.upress.umn.edu/9780816693146/program-earth/).

**Verified:** publisher title, author, date30March2016, description and contents; full book not read. The publisher links its own open Manifold edition; the exact edition's reuse licence was not reviewed.

**Contribution:** sensing changes which environmental relationships become noticeable and actionable. The book connects ecological, pollution and urban sensing. **BEOPS inference:** catalogue the sensing arrangement as well as the device: location choice, observer, sampling procedure and excluded phenomena. This is a conceptual basis for asking what remains invisible when the city is represented by accessible feeds. No local installation or dataset is implied.

## T02 — Instruments need practices and maintenance

Gabrys, J. (2019). *How to Do Things with Sensors*. University of Minnesota Press. [Publisher Manifold edition](https://manifold.umn.edu/projects/how-to-do-things-with-sensors). DOI[10.5749/9781452964096](https://doi.org/10.5749/9781452964096).

**Verified:** publisher description, contents and metadata; no full-book reading. Metadata explicitly gives CC BY-NC4.0 for this book, distinct from the licence of any sensor data mentioned within it.

**Contribution:** instruction, toolkits, networks and troubleshooting shape what participants can do with sensors. **BEOPS inference:** add operator/maintenance/calibration dependencies to a promising device record; successful installation alone cannot establish a sustained city sense. An interrupted signal may describe a broken sensing practice, not a physical absence in the city.

## T03 — Material traces can themselves convey evidence

Offenhuber, D. (2023). *Autographic Design: The Matter of Data in a Self-Inscribing World*. MIT Press. ISBN 9780262547024. [Primary publisher](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262547024/autographic-design/).

**Verified:** publisher gives publication19December2023, author, title and description; full book not read. No reuse licence established on this page.

**Contribution:** material traces and imprints can serve as evidence displays, making data production visible. **BEOPS inference:** do not exclude deposits on filters, water marks, wear, thermal traces or indicator materials merely because they are not digital endpoints. Their interpretation needs a documented observation procedure, reference and time interval. An image of a trace is an observation of that trace; inferred pollution history remains a separate interpretation.

## T04 — Telecom microwave links as rainfall observations

Overeem, A., Leijnse, H., & Uijlenhoet, R. (2013). Country-wide rainfall maps from cellular communication networks. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences,110*,2741–2745. DOI[10.1073/pnas.1217961110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1217961110). [KNMI author-institution record](https://www.knmi.nl/research/publications/country-wide-rainfall-maps-from-cellular-communication-networks); [TU Delft institutional record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid%3Ae8ca52bd-602c-423b-9383-41ee70662cc4).

**Verified:** primary institutional bibliographic record and repository abstract, not full paper. Dutch experiment; observed link attenuation supports derived rainfall maps, with a method described as applicable in real time. It does not establish a current public feed.

**BEOPS inference:** telecom infrastructure may contain weather evidence beyond explicit weather sensors. Belgrade microwave-link telemetry, operator cooperation, geometry, calibration and lawful access are unverified. Traffic content is unnecessary for this environmental method. Dataset licence not checked.

## T05 — A reproducible version of microwave-link rainfall retrieval

Overeem, A., Leijnse, H., & Uijlenhoet, R. (2016). Retrieval algorithm for rainfall mapping from microwave links in a cellular communication network. *Atmospheric Measurement Techniques,9*,2425–2444. DOI[10.5194/amt-9-2425-2016](https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-9-2425-2016). [Primary article page](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/9/2425/2016/).

**Verified:** publisher abstract, authors, dates and article licence CC BY3.0. Full PDF not read. The abstract describes15-minute rainfall maps from about2,400 links and an R implementation with a two-day dataset of about2,600 Dutch links. These are different stated sets and must not be conflated. Code/data availability is reported by the article; exact repository licences and files were not checked or downloaded.

**BEOPS inference:** treat this as an offline method candidate with published example data, then establish whether Serbian telemetry can lawfully supply equivalent fields. Fifteen-minute output cadence is not a measured current endpoint latency.

## T06 — Buried optical fibre as a distributed vibration instrument

Lindsey, N. J., Yuan, S., Lellouch, A., Gualtieri, L., Lecocq, T., & Biondi, B. (2020). City-Scale Dark Fiber DAS Measurements of Infrastructure Use During the COVID-19 Pandemic. *Geophysical Research Letters,47*(16),e2020GL089931. DOI[10.1029/2020GL089931](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020GL089931). [Primary publisher](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2020gl089931); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04861).

**Verified:** publisher author list, publication6August2020, abstract and data statement; full methods not reviewed. The Palo Alto experiment used optical-fibre DAS strain signals and template matching to derive traffic counts across a two-month2020 campaign. This is historical empirical support, not a currently operating Belgrade network.

**Data:** the paper explicitly links[Stanford deposit](https://purl.stanford.edu/bt630qc9349). The web opener returned an internal error at that deposit; availability and licence remain unresolved. Publisher's “Free Access” label does not establish a reusable data licence. **BEOPS inference:** investigate aggregate environmental vibration capabilities only after verifying local infrastructure, necessary interrogator and access. Do not adopt a paper's anonymity characterization as a blanket privacy guarantee.

## T07 — Air-filter archives can contain ecological information

Littlefair, J. E., Allerton, J. J., Brown, A. S., Butterfield, D. M., Robins, C., Economou, C. K., Garrett, N. R., & Clare, E. L. (2023). Air-quality networks collect environmental DNA with the potential to measure biodiversity at continental scales. *Current Biology,33*(11),R426–R428. DOI[10.1016/j.cub.2023.04.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.04.036).

**Verified:** [PubMed bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37279659/) plus[author-institution report](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/news/latest-news/2023/se/scientists-discover-air-quality-monitoring-stations-are-collecting-urgently-needed-biodiversity-data-.html), not full paper. Direct publisher route returned403; no bypass or alternate full-text retrieval was attempted. The primary institutional report describes analysis of air-monitoring filters from two UK locations, including archived filters.

**Contribution:** an existing sample can hold additional environmental evidence, revealed by a different laboratory method. **BEOPS inference:** investigate whether Belgrade air-sampling programmes retain suitable filters and metadata. Local eDNA practice, stored samples and data access are not established. Laboratory processing means this is not an immediate live biodiversity feed; species detection is not a direct population count. Article/data reuse licences unresolved.

## T08 — Sound contains more than a noise level

Pijanowski, B. C., Villanueva-Rivera, L. J., Dumyahn, S. L., Farina, A., Krause, B. L., Napoletano, B. M., Gage, S. H., & Pieretti, N. (2011). Soundscape Ecology: The Science of Sound in the Landscape. *BioScience,61*(3),203–216. DOI[10.1525/bio.2011.61.3.6](https://doi.org/10.1525/bio.2011.61.3.6). [Primary publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/article-abstract/61/3/203/238162).

**Verified:** publisher indexed abstract, full author list and issue metadata. DOI resolver failed in the web tool; the publisher's indexed record independently confirms it. Full text not read; no open reuse licence established.

**Contribution:** ecological soundscapes combine biological, geophysical and human-produced sound, with spatial and temporal structure. **BEOPS inference:** separate sound pressure, acoustic event classification and ecological interpretation. For Belgrade, environmental aggregates and appropriately scoped recordings would need their own access/privacy review; no speech recording or species abundance inference is authorized by this conceptual reference.
