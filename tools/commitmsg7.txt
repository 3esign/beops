Collect what we may, and find that the backlog was the wall

The question was whether everything permitted had actually been collected. It
had not: 32 sources carried a permission capture and exactly one had been
collected. The rest were samples in a scratch folder, which is not collection.

collect_permitted.py closes that, and it gates itself. Before any request it
reads the provenance ledger and takes the newest capture per source; a source is
collectable only if that capture says allowed and complete. Refused, undecided,
incomplete or never captured are all skipped with the reason printed, and there
is no flag to override it. On the first run it stored 183 files across eleven
sources and skipped 23 by its own gate, which is the tool working rather than
failing.

Then the backlog itself. The largest category in the registry was never a wall:
122 sources were neither refused nor absent, they simply had no capture, and our
own rule forbids collecting without one. capture_backlog.py cleared 121 of them
in one polite pass. The index went from 31 permitted and 137 undocumented to
143 permitted and 18 undocumented.

Four genuine refusals surfaced, and one of them is a pattern rather than a case:
api.openstreetmap.org disallows /api/, exactly as Wikidata disallows /sparql and
Overpass disallows /api/. Three major open-data services refuse the path that is
their API. The State Audit Institution and PVGIS disallow everything.

Two corrections, both about our own instruments rather than about any source.

C-005: the rule "take the stricter of the two engines" turned urllib's bug into
a refusal. jnportal.ujn.gov.rs publishes Allow: /$ , which RFC 9309 reads as a
permission and urllib, which does not implement the $ anchor, reads as a
refusal. The earlier rule was the wrong generalisation of a right observation -
urllib is unreliable in both directions, over-permitting where it ignores
longest-match and under-permitting where it ignores $. Now a disagreement makes
the verdict unknown, in both directions, and surfaces for a person. A safety
rule that always resolves ambiguity the same way is not conservative, it is
biased.

C-006: parking-servis.co.rs, the source of the OBS-001 pilot, came back
unverifiable. Its certificate is valid and chains to Sectigo Public Server
Authentication Root R46, which the machine trusts and the certifi bundle
shipped with this interpreter does not carry. Three further hosts were affected.
The fix exports this machine's own 121 trusted roots to a PEM in the repository
and prefers it, so the project's trust decision is a file that can be hashed,
committed and diffed rather than whatever a library happened to ship. Recorded
with it: the pilot collected from that source before the evidence-before-
collector rule existed, and the sequence was wrong even though the observation
stands.

Also written: the closed layer, an inventory of the 23 sources that are
genuinely unavailable, sorted into five kinds that must never be spoken of as
one - refused by name, open content behind a closed door, refused by letter but
permitted by intent, simply absent for Belgrade, and published once then
abandoned. Its central finding fell out of counting and survived being checked:
57 Serbian towns and municipalities publish open data, Novi Pazar publishes 67
datasets, and Belgrade publishes 7.
