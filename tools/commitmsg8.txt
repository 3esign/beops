Collect what we may, clear our own backlog, and scan the instruments that exist

The question was whether everything permitted had actually been collected. It
had not: thirty-two sources carried a permission capture and exactly one had
been collected; the rest were samples in a scratch folder, which is not
collection.

collect_permitted.py closes that and gates itself. Before any request it reads
the provenance ledger, takes the newest capture per source, and collects only
where that capture says allowed and complete. Refused, undecided, incomplete
and never-captured are skipped with the reason printed, and there is no flag to
override it. 218 files are now stored across thirteen sources with manifests
and hashes.

The largest category in the registry turned out not to be a wall at all. A
hundred and twenty-two sources were neither refused nor absent; they simply had
no capture, and our own rule forbids collecting without one. One polite pass
cleared 121 of them. The provenance index went from 31 permitted and 137
undocumented to 143 permitted and 18 undocumented. The backlog was the wall,
and it was ours.

Four genuine refusals surfaced and one of them is a pattern rather than a case:
OpenStreetMap's own API disallows /api/, exactly as Wikidata disallows /sparql
and Overpass disallows /api/. Three major open services refuse the path that is
their API.

Two corrections, both about our instruments rather than any source. Taking the
stricter of two robots engines turned urllib's failure to implement the dollar
anchor into a refusal of a site that permits us; a disagreement now yields
unknown in both directions and goes to a person, because a safety rule that
always resolves ambiguity the same way is not conservative, it is biased. And
the source of the parking pilot was reported unverifiable when its certificate
is valid and chains to a Sectigo root that this machine trusts and our shipped
CA bundle does not carry; the machine's own 121 roots are now exported to a PEM
in the repository, so the project's trust decision is a file that can be hashed
and diffed.

The discovery protocol writes down how the finds were actually made, so a census
can be repeated by someone else. Seven proven rules, eight new methods, and the
strongest is procurement as a sensor census: a city cannot install an instrument
without buying it, nor buy it without publishing a tender, so the procurement
record is an inventory of the instruments that exist including every one whose
data is never published. The registry gains the statuses a census needs and a
working project does not - paid, request_required, exists_unpublished,
exists_via_literature, and sought_not_found, the last always carrying what was
searched and when, because it is a claim about our method and must be
falsifiable.

Scanning for instruments that exist anywhere and then asking whether they exist
here found four new senses with Belgrade coverage. GBIF holds 512,241 dated,
geolocated records of living things in the city bounding box, twenty years deep.
iNaturalist holds 58,797 more, with the observer's own confidence attached.
Panoramax has Belgrade street imagery and declares CC-BY-SA-4.0 in the response
body, which matters because all three camera networks refused us. And adsb.lol
returns live aircraft over the city carrying outside air temperature, wind
direction and wind speed, so every aircraft above Belgrade is a free
atmospheric sonde.

One more absence of the now-familiar shape, checkable in a single file: of
1,535 GBFS bikeshare systems worldwide, Serbia has none, against Croatia's 22,
Romania's 11, Hungary's 10 and Bosnia's 3.

Evidence compressed from 1.5 GB to 332 MB. The sha256 in every manifest still
describes the original bytes as served, verifiable with gunzip.
